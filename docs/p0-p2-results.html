RustTunnel — P0–P2 Result Report

What was built, what was proven against a real network, and the one thing that is still unproven — which happens to be the thing the whole design rests on.

Draft v1 · 2026-08-08 · RustTunnel

Verdict

Five of eight tasks are done. The risk gate is not one of them. Everything that can be proven on one machine has been proven and filed. 2.2 — cross-NAT direct connection cannot be run here at all: both roles share one Wi-Fi interface, and loopback has nothing to traverse. Until 2.2 reports path=direct across two genuinely different networks, the premise of v2 is untested. Do not start P3.

Proven

The binary builds and runs. Identity persists and is owner-only. The wrong key is turned away. The path is reported and upgrades. The relayed fallback holds a link for five minutes without dropping.

Not proven

That a hole punch survives two real NATs. That the three-platform build is green on Linux and Windows. That a second machine can dial in.

Not built, by design

No data plane. No TCP or UDP forwarding, no reconnect, no service units. P0–P2 exists to answer "is this possible" before any of that is worth writing.

Where the plan's iroh sketch had drifted

The plan flagged its own API sketch as illustrative rather than compiled, and it was right to. Three things differ in the released 1.0.3.

The plan expected1.0.3 actually hasConsequence
A connection-type accessor to log direct vs relayed No conn_type at all. Path state is Connection::paths() for a snapshot plus a path_events() stream of PathEvent::{Opened, Closed, Selected, Lagged}. Path reporting reads TransportAddr::is_relay() off the selected path. Richer than expected — the address and RTT come along for free.
A relay mode to force the relayed path for 2.3 Builder::clear_ip_transports(). RelayMode exists but selects which relay, not whether direct paths are allowed. --relay-only drops the IP transports, so no direct path can ever be selected. Stronger than a preference — it is structural.
Endpoint::connect(endpoint_id, …) Same shape; EndpointId converts into EndpointAddr, and discovery fills in the addresses. None. The four-line sketch compiled essentially as written.

Why the path is restated once a minute. 2.3 asks for path=relayed at both ends of a five-minute window. A single establishment line plus the absence of an upgrade event would have been an inference, not evidence — it asks the reader to trust that a missing log line means nothing changed. A per-minute still up path=… line makes the claim directly readable, and it is what 5.3's 24-hour soak will need anyway.

The gate, and what it needs

This is the only outstanding item that can change the design rather than merely delay it.

# home box, on the home ISP
rtun id                                   # -> ID_HOME
rtun serve --tcp 22 --allow $ID_LAPTOP 2>&1 | tee serve.log

# laptop, tethered to a phone on cellular — NOT the home Wi-Fi
rtun id                                   # -> ID_LAPTOP
rtun connect $ID_HOME --tcp 2222:22 2>&1 | tee connect.log

Pass condition: both logs independently reach a path selected … path=direct line. The path=relayed establishment line before it is expected — wait for the upgrade.

If it stays relayed on both sides, stop. Try a third network before concluding anything — one symmetric NAT is survivable and two are not. If a third network also fails, hole punching is not available on the networks you have, and the honest options are keeping v1's relay design or accepting relayed operation and giving up the latency and bandwidth argument. The numbers in the table above are what that concession costs: roughly 200× the round-trip time.

How the in-process check was kept honest

The plan's one check lists five cases; three of them exercise forwarding that does not exist yet. Two are live:

CheckCovers
allowlist_admits_only_listed_peersAn unlisted key is refused and a listed one is admitted — one of the two authorization cases the plan says earn their place, because a regression there is an open door rather than a broken feature.
port_map_parses_and_rejects_junkThe local:remote parser, including port zero, out-of-range and missing halves.
Both checks are fully offline. They bind 127.0.0.1:0 with iroh's presets::Minimal — no relay, no discovery, no DNS. The suite runs in about two seconds and the CI runners need no egress, which removes the most common source of a flaky cross-platform job before the job has ever run.
